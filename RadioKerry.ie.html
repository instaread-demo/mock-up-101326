Tralee not included in first phase of government programme to deliver student accommodation

Oct 12, 2026 13:33
By radiokerrynews
Share this article

Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 1:45
Instaread

Tralee is not included in the first phase of the government’s programme to secure additional student accommodation.

The update was provided in response to a parliamentary question from Kerry Fianna Fáil TD Michael Cahill.

Deputy President of the MTU Student’s Union for Kerry, Patrick McGrath, had criticised last week’s budget, as it did not include any funding for publicly-owned student accommodation.

Advertisement

Deputy Cahill asked the Minister for Further and Higher Education, Research, Innovation and Science James Lawless to provide funding for public-owned college student accommodation in Tralee.

Last week, the MTU Students’ Union Deputy President for Kerry, Patrick McGrath criticised the omission of publicly-funded student accommodation in this year’s budget.

He said there are currently no publicly-owned student beds in the county.

Advertisement

Responding to Deputy Cahill, Minister James Lawless said a programme is being advanced which will assist higher education institutions in securing additional purpose-built student accommodation capacity through private development.

He said this will deliver accommodation on or near campus via nomination agreements, licensed sites, and site-servicing arrangements.

Minister Lawless said the initial phase of the programme focuses on the larger university centres of Dublin, Cork, and Galway.

Advertisement

He said subsequent phases will extend to a number of regional locations including Tralee, ensuring student accommodation requirements across the country are considered as the programme develops.

Minister Lawless also said the government supports additional supply through the Rent-a-Room Scheme, through which 561 rooms were being advertised in Kerry at the end of September.

Deputy Michael Cahill says students in Tralee should not be left entirely dependent on the private rental market to find somewhere to live while pursuing their education.

Advertisement

He said the government must ensure the needs of regional towns are not overlooked in favour of larger cities.
