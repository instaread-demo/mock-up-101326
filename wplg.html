Broward mayor denied access to BSO training facility as he, Tony spar over budget

BROWARD COUNTY, Fla. — Broward Mayor Mark Bogen followed through Monday on a promise to try to tour the Broward Sheriff’s Office training center as he and other commissioners spar with Sheriff Gregory Tony over the budget.

Leer en español

About 20 minutes after Bogen, with cameras rolling, tried to visit the BSO training gym, he was denied access.

“You’re keeping me out (of) here, right?” Bogen said.

Tony spoke to the media for the first time since the battle with the county over his budget began making headlines.

“We’re already at one of the most recognized and notable conflicts amongst county government and sheriff’s office,” Tony said, later adding, “I will not allow this organization to be bullied or intimidated.”

BSO controls the building and can deny access. But the commission says that after approving a nearly $900 million core budget for the agency, commissioners want to see inside and how the money is used.

“It’s the gymnasium, the sauna, spa, massage, cold plunge and all the excess amenities that are there,” Bogen said. “Residents of Broward County should see where their money is going.”

In a new twist, the sheriff told Local 10 News reporter Janine Stanwood that the public should know about new allegations.

“Janine, he also mentioned on your show he had two wonderful lunch meetings with me,” Tony said.

The sheriff claims that was when he requested raises for his deputies in late June. In response, he says, the mayor sent him a letter asking for a donation to a political action committee.

Bogen says that characterization is untrue, telling Local 10 News on the phone that the top cop is a “compulsive liar.”

All this comes as commissioners weigh how much BSO should control.

They recently voted to return the fire department to civilian control and hired a firm to study whether to install a new police force, rather than BSO, to guard the airport and seaport.

Afterward, commissioners were all sent subpoenas requesting information on the probe.

Local 10 News reached out to state prosecutors and was waiting to hear back as of Monday afternoon.

Bogen said he and commissioners plan to hold a 9 a.m. vote on Tuesday on whether to take judicial action to get inside the training facility.
